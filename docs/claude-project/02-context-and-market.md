# 02 — Context, Market & Audiences

## The pilot neighborhood

- **Location:** Parque Industrial, Comuna del Café, Pereira, Risaralda, Colombia.
- **Population reach:** 30,000+ inhabitants.
- **Character:** Dense, working-class / popular-economy neighborhood with a high concentration of small and informal businesses operated by and for residents.

Pereira sits in Colombia's Eje Cafetero (coffee region). The neighborhood has strong local identity but limited digital representation — most commerce happens through word of mouth, WhatsApp, and physical signage.

## What is the "popular economy" (economía popular)?

The popular economy is the dense web of small-scale, often informal economic activity that sustains low- and middle-income neighborhoods: corner stores (tiendas), home kitchens, hairdressers, mechanics, seamstresses, street vendors, small repair shops, micro-services. It is:

- **Real and large** — a major share of employment and daily commerce.
- **Mostly informal** — frequently without RUT, Cámara de Comercio registration, or formal premises.
- **Relationship-driven** — built on trust, proximity, and reputation.
- **Digitally underserved** — excluded by platforms that assume formality, bank accounts, and high digital literacy.

BarrioRed is built *for* this economy, not adapted from a tool built for formal SMEs.

## Audiences & personas

### 1. The informal/small merchant — "Doña Marta, la tienda de la esquina"
- Runs a small or informal business, often from home or a modest storefront.
- Sells via WhatsApp and walk-bys; has no website and isn't on maps.
- Low-to-medium digital literacy; uses a smartphone mainly for WhatsApp and Facebook.
- **Needs:** more visibility, more customers, legitimacy — without paperwork or cost.
- **Wins with BarrioRed:** a free, dignified profile with photos, hours, location, and a WhatsApp button; appears in the barrio's directory and map.
- **Onboarding friction to respect:** distrust of "apps," fear of cost/scams, limited time, limited data.

### 2. The resident / neighbor — "Andrés, vecino del barrio"
- Lives in the neighborhood; wants to support and find local options.
- Relies on neighborhood WhatsApp/Facebook groups for news and recommendations.
- **Needs:** find nearby businesses, know what's happening (alerts, events), buy/sell locally, reach services.
- **Wins with BarrioRed:** one trustworthy local hub instead of scattered chat groups.

### 3. The community leader / organization — "Junta de Acción Comunal, líderes, fundaciones"
- Organizes the neighborhood; trusted intermediaries.
- **Needs:** channels to inform and mobilize residents; tools that strengthen the community.
- **Role for BarrioRed:** key partners for adoption, legitimacy, and content (alerts, events, services).

### 4. Local government / institutions
- Comuna and municipal entities, public-service providers.
- **Role:** sources of the services directory, potential partners, potential funders.

### 5. The platform operator (internal)
- Super admins (platform-wide) and community admins/moderators (per neighborhood).
- **Needs:** moderation, onboarding support, content curation, analytics.

## Why now

- Near-universal smartphone + WhatsApp penetration in these neighborhoods.
- A clear gap: no platform serves informal commerce + neighborhood life together, hyperlocally.
- Low infrastructure cost makes a lean, sustainable model viable in low-income markets.

## Competitive landscape (how we're different)

| Alternative | Why it underserves the popular economy |
|---|---|
| Google Maps / Business | Requires formal-ish setup; informal businesses rarely listed; no neighborhood layer. |
| Delivery apps (Rappi, etc.) | Commissions, formality, device/logistics requirements; exclude micro/informal. |
| Facebook / WhatsApp groups | Fragmented, noisy, no structure, no directory/map, content disappears. |
| City-wide classifieds | Not hyperlocal; no trust layer; no informal-business inclusion. |
| Generic business directories | Built for formal SMEs; no community, alerts, or WhatsApp-first design. |

**BarrioRed's wedge:** the only platform that combines *informal-business inclusion + WhatsApp-first + hyperlocal trust + neighborhood social layer*, all replicable and low-cost.

## Constraints to design around

- **Low data / low-end devices** → mobile-first, lightweight, PWA, offline-tolerant.
- **Low digital literacy / trust barriers** → simple flows, human onboarding, no jargon.
- **Informality** → never require RUT/Cámara de Comercio for basic participation.
- **Low willingness/ability to pay** → free core; monetization is light and optional.
- **Spanish, Colombian, neighborhood register** → all user-facing content.
